# Product lens
Score: 6 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- The one thing is clear: a bedside object that replaces the phone for alarm and tomorrow. It passes the one-sentence test.
- The experience starts from the moment of use. Two eyes at rest, a tap turns them into the colon of the clock. That is a memorable signature, and it comes from movement, not a cartoon face.
- Taste is visible in the rules: no mouth, no feeds, no notification mirroring, a small palette, one typeface, calm copy. Saying no is already built in.
- The honesty rules (estimates only, not a medical device) protect the brand from the first ad.
- A working interactive prototype exists, so the experience can be judged before hardware arrives.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **The product is a screen with a personality, but the job is waking up reliably.** If the alarm ever fails (Wi-Fi drop, crash, no way to silence it without looking), trust is gone. Physical buttons are still an open question. No buttons, no haptics confirmed, and the alarm is the core promise.
2. **The experience has seams the founders do not own.** The briefing depends on a phone app, calendar OAuth, a cloud or backend for TTS, and weather. Setup is the first five minutes the customer has. The app is unfinished, the protocol is undecided, and the whole thing breaks if any hand-off does.
3. **The craft promise versus the cost target.** Timber body, flush display, speaker, haptics, sensors, at under about EUR 40 is unverified. Pressure to cut will land on the parts nobody sees (speaker, haptic motor, finish), which are exactly what a premium bedside object is judged on. The prototype board is also not the product, so the real object is still unproven.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What does the first five minutes look like, from unboxing to the first morning briefing, and how many steps is it?
- What happens when Wi-Fi is down at 6:30? Does the alarm still wake the user?
- How is the alarm silenced half asleep, with the eyes closed?
- Is the haptic pulse real hardware on the prototype or a promise on the site?
- Which of the features would you cut first: Ask NOCTIS, room sensing, or the sleep estimate?
- Who owns the briefing when it is wrong or late?
- Why is the price EUR 120, and what does the customer get at that price that they do not get from a phone?
- Is there a real unit cost quote for the board, speaker and timber, or only the target?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
- Alarm works fully offline, with a defined physical or touch-free way to stop and snooze it.
- Hardware tested: confirm the exact board, then confirm haptics, speaker and microphone on it before any further promise on the site.
- A written setup flow of a few steps, tested with strangers, before the app is called done.
- A cost quote for the full bill of materials, with the craft items (timber, speaker, haptic) named, not the target alone.
- Cut anything that does not serve alarm and briefing until those two are flawless.
