# Monopoly lens
Score: 3/10 | Vote: PASS

## What is strong
- Clear, narrow starting niche: design-aware adults who want the phone out of the bedroom. A small market that could be owned first.
- The eyes-to-colon interaction is a distinctive piece of design. It is a real, ownable brand asset, but design, not technology.
- Direct-to-consumer with a live waitlist gives a distribution start, though its size is unknown.
- Hard rules (no feeds, no medical claims, on-device mic) are a coherent position and avoid regulatory trouble.
- Timing has a plausible story: phone-at-night fatigue. Whether it is strong enough is unproven.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No durable advantage.** The brief shows no proprietary technology, no network effect, no scale economics. It runs on a stock Waveshare ESP32-S3 board with cloud TTS. A copycat with the same board can ship a similar product in month six. Brand and taste are the only moat, and brand is not yet built.
2. **Distribution is unproven.** The brief names a waitlist but gives no size, no source of traffic, and no acquisition cost. Open question: how do the first 100 buyers arrive, and at what cost each?
3. **Competing with the phone, which is free.** The buyer already owns a device that does the alarm. EUR 120 must beat a zero-cost default, plus smart speakers and alarm clocks already on the table. The "secret" is not stated, and the product reads as incremental, not 10x better.

Also unresolved: unverified EUR 40 unit cost, no manufacturer, no CE/RED certification, an unfinished app. Any of these can end the company before the moat question matters.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What do you know about bedside behaviour that others do not? What is the secret?
- What stops a copycat in month six, and what do you own that cannot be copied?
- How many people are on the waitlist, where did they come from, and how many would pay EUR 120?
- What is the tightest segment you can dominate first, and how big is it?
- Who else sells to this buyer today (alarm clocks, smart displays, sleep devices), and why do they lose?
- What does the phone-in-another-room buyer give up, and will they truly do it?
- Where does the company stand in ten years if a large platform adds this?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
Not applicable, vote is PASS. To reconsider: evidence of a real 10x edge or a defensible brand with measured demand, a verified unit cost and a named manufacturer, and a tested channel with a known cost per first 100 customers.
